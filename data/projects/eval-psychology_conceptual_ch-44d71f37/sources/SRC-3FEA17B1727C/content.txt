Learning objectives:
- Explain how classical conditioning occurs.
- Summarize acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, and discrimination.

Relevant content summary:
Classical conditioning is learning through associations between stimuli, allowing an organism to anticipate events. A neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus after repeated pairing with an unconditioned stimulus. Acquisition is the initial period during which the association is learned, and timing between the stimuli matters. Examples should distinguish the unconditioned stimulus and response from the conditioned stimulus and response. Instruction should also help learners differentiate extinction, spontaneous recovery, stimulus generalization, and discrimination instead of treating all post-acquisition behavior as the same process.
